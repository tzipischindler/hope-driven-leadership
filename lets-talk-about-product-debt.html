Let's Talk About Product Debt

Over the years, I've learned how crucial it is to have honest discussions about the debt we’re carrying, understanding the cost of that debt and how to prioritize it against feature work.

Balance

Finding the balance between solving things for our customers, improving their experience, and solving things for us, so we can continue to develop the product. In reality, we’re balancing value, risk, and effort to understand priorities and make tough calls about what we do now and what we do later.

Collaboration

As time goes by, debt can become more complex and heavier. This often means that only through collaboration between teams can we solve it. This asks us to listen and think with openness as well as criticality, while respecting the diverse perspectives and needs brought to the table, reaching convergence and moving forward with a plan. This is what we can live with for now; this is what we hope and plan for the future.

How do we know what to do now, and what later

When we ran into compatibility issues with our installer tech, this was my biggest trial up to that point, and it was part of finding that balance and starting to plan for a future where we could pay off a huge debt we’d been deprioritizing for years.

I didn’t have one tool to do that then, but what I did have were healthy discussions with my teams around the product debt we carry and when, or if, we want to address it.

Recently I’ve been intrigued to take part in a Hackathon that was all about learning how to use AI to build solutions. I took it as an opportunity to dive back into this topic and started talking with teams about how they experience technical debt. I found a commonality among my interviewees: how open communication, transparency, and talking about it are important. My own personal motivation here was wanting to find a way to prioritize this work against feature work, while setting KPIs to measure our impact.

What I hope will come of this is a tool that will encourage teams to have these conversations early on, before that product debt has a negative impact on how we work in teams, on end users, and/or impedes the business from pursuing its mission or vision.

I believe that changing our narrative from seeing this as a technical problem to learning how it impacts the business can unlock new possibilities. For example, making a call on a venture we tried and declaring it a failure, or setting criteria for what defines success for us as a product, thus avoiding carrying on the weight of bad code and workarounds, unmaintained systems, and outdated tech.
